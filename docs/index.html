The affected hierarchy, in one paragraph

Eight eMudhra intermediate CA keys were compromised (EM DV/OV/EV TLS CA - G3B/G3C plus two code-signing CAs). They were cross-signed by emSign ECC Root CA - G3 (trusted by Chrome, Mozilla, Microsoft) and by a Sectigo AAA Certificate Services cross-sign (now revoked/removed). This scan found no Indian gov site on those intermediates. What it did find: 223 gov domains serving certificates from eMudhra's G1 family (EM * TLS CA - G2A-1 → emSign Root TLS CA - G1 → legacy emSign Root CA - G1, and emSign SSL CA - G1 → emSign Root CA - G1). That family remains trusted today, but eMudhra is exiting the public TLS business and Chrome is evaluating G1's future — reissue from another CA is the safe move.

Methodology

  • Universe: registry dump of 9,670 registrable .gov.in/.nic.in domains; per domain the apex and www. were probed on :443 (fallback :8443), plus a common-prefix vhost probe (portal/services/sso/apps/…) over impacted domains.
  • Chains captured with openssl s_client -showcerts; a host is flagged when its leaf issuer or any presented chain element matches the compromised intermediates or carries eMudhra/emSign branding.
  • Verdict G1-FAMILY (watch-list) = issued under emSign Root CA - G1 / emSign Root TLS CA - G1; CONFIRMED-COMPROMISED-INT = one of the 8 compromised intermediates (none found).
  • Limitations: HTTPS on 443/8443 only; full CT-based vhost enumeration is pending (crt.sh rate limits); universe is the registry snapshot, not a live registry.

If you operate one of these sites

  1. Check your chain: openssl s_client -connect yourhost:443 -servername yourhost -showcerts | openssl x509 -noout -issuer.
  2. Reissue from a CA outside the eMudhra/emSign family and redeploy. Update clients too (ca-certificates, certifi ≥ 2026.10.19, Go ≥ 1.27.3, Node ≥ 22.23.4; rebuild containers older than 2026-10-20).
  3. Indian gov sites on CCA-India chains (NIC sub-CAs) are a separate matter — those roots are not in public trust stores and are unaffected by this incident.
